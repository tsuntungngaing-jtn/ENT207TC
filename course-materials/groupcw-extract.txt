2. PROJECT SELECTION & GROUP FORMATION 
Finding Your Project 
In Weeks 1–2, each group selects a real-world problem to solve with a digital solution that can realistically be built 
within the semester. While most teams are expected to build apps, other digital solutions are acceptable if they 
address an industry-relevant challenge and demonstrate technical depth (e.g., intelligent agents, interactive 
dashboards, AI tools, or advanced web applications). Static or overly simple solutions do not qualify. 
Group Formation 
Groups are formed with approximately seven members, based on project interest. Project fields may include (but are 
not limited to): AFCT, AIAC, IME, IOT, IRE, IFB, CHIPS, EEH, and other emerging areas. 
Validation Process 
1. Brainstorming — generate multiple problem ideas and propose digital solutions. 
2. Peer Feedback — present ideas to another group using the Green / Yellow / Red system. 
3. Faculty Approval — EEH Mentors or panel review final ideas. Rejected ideas must pivot and resubmit. 
GREEN 
Clear problem + realistic and buildable solution. Proceeds to development. 
YELLOW 
Valid problem but solution needs more clarity or scoping. Revise and resubmit. 
RED

3. COMPONENT DETAILS 
NOTE: Each team is expected to engage with at least one External Mentor during the semester (XEC faculty, 
industry practitioner, alumnus, or domain expert). Document this using the External Mentor Feedback Form 
(Form 3 of the Module Handbook) by end of Week 9 — at least one submission per semester is required. This is 
scored once (not weekly) under the "External Mentor" criterion in the Weekly Process rubric below. 
NOTE: "Features" referenced throughout this brief (e.g., "three core features") should follow the guidance in 
Appendix A: Acceptable Features for Projects. Login-only functionality, static/landing pages, generic contact 
forms, and visual polish without underlying logic do not count as features. 
Component 1 (40%): Weekly Process 
The project is completed through weekly milestones. Each week builds toward the group's overall deliverables, with 
documented evidence of progress. Teams should maintain an active Kanban board throughout the semester — this is 
verified as evidence of how the team organises and executes work. 
To establish a transparent individual contribution trail, students are strongly encouraged to use the module's digital 
workspace portal, where card movements and digital proofs are aggregated automatically. Students choosing the 
manual route must carefully document their own weekly individual contributions. Each week's Form 1 log also 
includes a short individual reflection field for every team member.

Component 3 (20%): Demo Day 
Demo Day is the culmination of your startup journey. Each team delivers a live presentation and Q&A, showcasing 
their working application to an audience of peers, mentors, and invited guests. 
Demo Day Deliverables 
• Live demonstration of the app with real functionality. 
• Presentation slides (PPTX or PDF) — clear, narrative-driven, audience-ready. 
• App access for assessors (published link / test account / APK / repository with README). 
• Demo Day Live Recording — the official recording captured during the event (the team is responsible to record). 
Submitted with your Component 3 package. 
• Professional Video Submission — a separately produced video by your team, edited with subtitles, professional 
quality, representing your best public-facing pitch. Upload as MP4. 
Minimum Requirements for Digital Solutions 
To qualify for Demo Day, each team's solution must include: 
• A user-facing interface (app, dashboard, or web tool). 
• User input and interaction (form, voice, chat, or data upload). 
• At least five screens with logical, structured navigation. 
• User authentication (login functionality). 
• Three core features or functions (see Appendix A for what counts as a feature). 
• Support for real user testing (minimum 10 users). 
• A clearly defined use case and demonstrable technical value. 
• Your demonstration of your working digital prototype must satisfy the interactive simulation requirement. As 
part of the live demonstration, walk through a realistic user scenario simulation within your app — the inputs, the 
interaction, the resulting output — and explain how that insight would inform a real product, market, or 
operational decision for your venture.

Component 4 (10%): Journey Reflection 
We want honest, specific reflection about the format, support received, tools used, and the learning experience — 
plus ideas for improvement. Minimum 900 meaningful words total. One upload per group at the end of the semester. 
No references required. This reflection should draw on and build from each member's individual weekly reflections 
submitted through the semester. 
Please address the following prompts (2–4 sentences each): 
• Format & flow: What worked or did not in the startup studio model? 
• EEH support: What support from EEH (Mentors, workspace, feedback) helped? What was missing? 
• School support: What support from your home school helped? What was missing? 
• Mentor support: One concrete example where your EEH Mentor materially helped — or could have. 
• TA support: Was the TA check-in process useful? What would have improved it? 
• AI Agents: Did you use AI agents in your product or workflow? What value did they add, or not? 
• Kanban: Did the Kanban board help your team's organization? What would you change? 
• Learning environment: Did sessions feel safe, inclusive, and collaborative? One enabler and one blocker. 
• Barriers & enablers: Top two blockers and how you got past them; top two supports that mattered. 
• Two low-effort fixes we can implement for future students.

APPENDIX A: ACCEPTABLE FEATURES FOR PROJECTS 
The list below illustrates types of features that are acceptable and meaningful in ENT207TC (Module Handbook, 
Section 21). These are here to inspire, not to restrict. What matters is that every feature directly helps solve a real 
problem for your intended users. You are not building for practice — you are building to make a difference. 
Navigation & Access 
• Guest access with limited functionality 
• Role-based access (admin, user, mentor, etc.) 
• Structured user flows with access-specific content 
User Interaction Features 
• Customizable profile that influences app functionality 
• Dynamic task or goal tracker with progress logic 
• Smart calendar or reminder tied to user input or behavior 
• Gamified progress indicators (badges, levels, streaks) 
AI & Smart Features 
• Chatbot trained on specific user needs or data 
• AI-generated suggestions that adapt over time 
• AI agents that perform tasks autonomously within your app 
• Input analysis with personalized recommendations 
• Auto-generated summaries or reflections from user content 
Logic, Data & Feedback Systems 
• User-facing database features (filter, sort, search) 
• Real-time leaderboard or comparison system 
• Analytics dashboard that visualizes trends 
• Systems that evolve based on cumulative user activity 
Advanced Features 
• Integrations that add real functionality (AI, finance, weather, IoT) 
• Secure and useful use of OpenAI, DeepSeek, Gemini, or similar models 
• APIs offering content, evaluation, or communication support 
• Webhooks or external automations supporting your use case
