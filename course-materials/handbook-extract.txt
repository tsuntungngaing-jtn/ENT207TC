SECTION A: BASIC INFORMATION 
1. Welcome to the Digital Startup Lab 
Welcome to ENT207TC Digital Startup Lab! This module is a hands-on, challenge-based journey 
where you and your team will learn how to take an idea from concept to a working digital product, just 
like a real-world startup team. 
The Digital Startup Lab is intentionally designed as a co-learning environment. While the module 
leaders will provide structure, guidance, and feedback, we will also explore new tools, technologies, 
and startup challenges together. In fast-moving fields like digital entrepreneurship, knowing how to 
learn, adapt, and experiment matters more than having all the answers. 
One of your key resources is your EEH Mentor, a high-quality mentor here to help you think deeper, 
connect to relevant expertise, and navigate the challenges of building a real product. Administrative 
coordination and progress tracking is handled by Teaching Assistants (TAs), who will help ensure 
your team stays on track week by week.

4. Module Description 
Aims and Fit of the Module 
This module is designed to give students a comprehensive foundation in the modern startup 
ecosystem. It aims to: 
• 
Provide an in-depth understanding of technological advancements relevant to digital 
startups, including artificial intelligence, AI agents, no-code and low-code platforms, and 
emerging technologies. 
• 
Equip students with essential skills to navigate the landscape of digital startups, integrating 
technology-driven solutions while understanding ecosystem components, resources, and 
support networks. 
• 
Cultivate emotional intelligence and resilience crucial for entrepreneurs, enabling students to 
manage the complex challenges inherent in digital entrepreneurship. 
• 
Introduce students to structured product development through milestone-based learning, 
Kanban-style workflow management, and iterative prototyping. 
• 
Develop the ability to use AI agents as intelligent tools to augment startup workflows and 
product features.

12. Educational Philosophy 
Think of this lab not as a classroom, but as a startup studio. You are not just students, you are 
designers, founders, analysts, and creators. Let's build things that matter, and learn what we do not 
know yet, together. 
Over the semester, you will experience the full digital startup cycle: from identifying a meaningful 
problem, brainstorming creative solutions, building prototypes, promoting your app, gathering user 
feedback, and pitching your product at Demo Day. 
We use three interconnected learning philosophies: 
• 
Constructionist learning: by building real prototypes, you learn by doing. 
• 
Facilitative teaching: we act as mentors, sounding boards, and co-explorers more than 
traditional lecturers. 
• 
Heutagogy (self-directed learning): you take ownership of your learning path, choosing 
technologies and directions that suit your interests and goals. 
 
13. What Will You Do? 
1. From idea to solution: you will identify problems worth solving, explore the problem-solution 
space, and frame issues that inspire innovation. 
2. Sketch the solution: you will brainstorm multiple approaches and sketch low-fidelity 
wireframes showing your core features. 
3. Building a digital version: you will transform sketches into interactive digital prototypes using 
design thinking and UX principles. 
4. Create an app:  you will use platforms and code of your choice to build a minimum viable 
product (MVP) app. 
5. Work with AI agents: you will explore how intelligent agents can augment your startup product 
and automate specific workflows. 
6. Promo & launch campaign: you will create a promotional package including a 1-minute video, 
landing page, and visuals, and you will use them to find real users to test your app. 
7. User testing and feedback: you will test with real users, collect structured feedback, and iterate. 
8. Demo Day: you will showcase your product in a public pitch event and submit a professional-
quality video. 
 
14. Assessment Components — Details 
The Weekly Process component evaluates how the team worked throughout the semester, while the 
Final Deliverables evaluates the quality and completeness of the final project outputs and supporting 
evidence. Think of the Process component as assessing how your team built the project, and the Final 
Deliverables as assessing what your team ultimately produced. The Demo Day will be the 
demonstration of your work.  
Weekly Process (40%) 
Team progress is evaluated through weekly milestone deliverables and consistent workflow tracking.  
To establish a transparent individual contribution trail, students are highly encouraged to carefully 
document their weekly individual contributions. 
Each group should submit 2 Drafts: Draft 1 (Week 4) and Draft 2 (Week 9).  
Draft 1 is submitted in Week 4 and covers the digital prototype report, documenting the problem, 
solution concept, chosen features.

Draft 2 is submitted in Week 9 and covers tech stack choice, the promotion report and user testing

Both drafts form part of the Final Deliverables and must be submitted in the LM. Submitting on time 
matters, these drafts are the evidence trail that supports your final grade. More specific, Draft 1 
becomes the basis for your final Digital Prototype Report, and Draft 2 becomes the basis for your final 
Promotion Report and User Testing Report; you are refining the same documents, not producing new 
ones. 
Each group will maintain a Kanban board throughout the semester. We will verify the board activity. 
Kanban is not just a tool; it is evidence of how your team organizes and executes work. 
Final Deliverables (30%) 
The Final Deliverables is a curated record of your team's project journey, submitted at the end of the 
semester. It must include: 
• 
Checkpoint 1 Report (End of Week 3): problem choice, problem validation, solution concept, 
digital prototype (the digital prototype and set of chosen features). 
• 
Checkpoint 2 Report (End of Week 7): tech stack rationale, working app with 3 features, 
Kanban evidence, building reflection. 
• 
Checkpoint 3 Report (End of Week 9): promotion evidence (channels, reach, engagement), 
user testing data (10+ testers), bug fixes implemented, launch readiness self-assessment. 
• 
Digital Prototype Report (End of Week 10): final product documentation — architecture 
overview, feature set, tech stack, access instructions for assessors, known limitations. 
• 
Promotion Report (End of Week 10): channels used (with dates), materials deployed (video 
link, landing page), reach and engagement metrics, testers onboarded, what worked / what did 
not, timestamped evidence screenshots. 
• 
User Testing Report (End of Week 10): structured report on 10+ real users — testing 
methodology, key findings, user quotes or data, changes implemented based on feedback. 
Demo Day (20%) 
Demo Day is the culmination of your startup journey. Each team will: 
• 
Deliver a live demonstration and Q&A in front of peers, mentors, and invited guests. 
• 
Submit the live recording of Demo Day. The team is responsible for recording during Demo 
Day. 
• 
Submit a professionally edited demo video, created and edited by your team, with subtitles and 
production quality. This should represent your best pitch to the world. 
• 
Your demonstration of your working digital prototype must satisfy the interactive simulation 
requirement. As part of the live demonstration, walk through a realistic user scenario 
simulation within your app — the inputs, the interaction, the resulting output — and explain 
how that insight would inform a real product, market, or operational decision for your venture. 
Journey Reflection (10%) 
A group reflection (minimum 900 meaningful words) covering the module's format, support received, 
tools used, challenges faced, and suggestions for improvement. One upload per group.

Final Review  
All members of a group normally receive the same group mark for each component. Where 
accumulated process evidence such as Kanban history, weekly milestone submissions, drafts, 
checkpoints, TA check-in notes, and Mentor observations clearly show that a student's contribution 
was substantially different from their teammates', Module Leaders and EEH Mentors may adjust that 
student's individual mark relative to the group mark. Any such adjustment is capped at a maximum of 
10 percentage points, and is decided by Module Leaders and/or EEH Mentors based on process 
evidence.

17. Academic Integrity 
Offences of plagiarism, cheating, or fabrication can result in attendance at a University-level 
committee and significant penalties. Please read the Academic Integrity Policy available on e-Bridge

The use of AI tools is permitted and encouraged in this module as part of the authentic learning 
experience. However, all AI-assisted work must be disclosed, and the intellectual contribution of your 
team must be evident. Using AI to complete work without understanding or contributing to it is treated 
as a form of academic dishonesty.

All submission rules and late submission penalties are governed by the University's Code of Practice 
for Assessment, available on e-Bridge. Late submissions will incur a 5% deduction per day, up to five 
days. Submissions beyond five days are not accepted.

21. Feature Guidelines 
The list below illustrates types of features that are acceptable and meaningful in ENT207TC. These are 
here to inspire, not to restrict. What matters is that every feature directly helps solve a real problem for 
your intended users. You are not building for practice — you are building to make a difference. 
Navigation & Access 
• 
Guest access with limited functionality 
• 
Role-based access (admin, user, mentor, etc.) 
• 
Structured user flows with access-specific content 
User Interaction Features 
• 
Customizable profile that influences app functionality 
• 
Dynamic task or goal tracker with progress logic 
• 
Smart calendar or reminder tied to user input or behavior 
• 
Gamified progress indicators (badges, levels, streaks) 
AI & Smart Features 
• 
Chatbot trained on specific user needs or data 
• 
AI-generated suggestions that adapt over time 
• 
AI agents that perform tasks autonomously within your app 
• 
Input analysis with personalized recommendations 
• 
Auto-generated summaries or reflections from user content

• 
User-facing database features (filter, sort, search)
